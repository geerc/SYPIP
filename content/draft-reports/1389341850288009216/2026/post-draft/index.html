#10 kcoffield

Projected starter points per game: 115.0

Biggest Reach: Colston Loveland — pick 36, ADP 61.0 (+25.0)

Biggest Value: Malik Willis — pick 116, ADP 99.0 (-17.0)

This last-place build has a genuine superflex spine—Jalen Hurts, Baker Mayfield, and ADP bargain Malik Willis give kcoffield three playable quarterbacks, while Jefferson, DeVonta Smith, and Jameson Williams make the WR room legitimately terrifying. 1 But managed lineup is not best ball: seven receivers cannot rescue a roster starting two RBs with only Cook, Etienne, and Pollard behind them, so one injury or workload wobble turns the flex spot into a weekly garage sale. Cook is the clear lifeboat, with current half-PPR sources treating him far above Etienne and Pollard, but that RB depth chart is still a paper bridge over a volcano. 2 Loveland may be an elite tight-end bet, yet taking him 25 picks ahead of this league-adjusted ADP was a full-speed reach when the roster desperately needed running-back insulation; Oronde Gadsden is not enough cover. 3 The Malik Willis ADP value softens the blow, but it cannot hide the draft-night crime scene: awesome top-end pass catchers, flimsy RB construction, and absolutely no margin for error.

Biggest Value: Bo Nix — pick 70, ADP 29.0 (-41.0)

#8 Stinky Nala

Projected starter points per game: 118.3

Biggest Reach: Denver Broncos — pick 115, ADP 143.0 (+28.0)

Biggest Value: Dak Prescott — pick 66, ADP 16.0 (-50.0)

Dak at pick 66 versus a 16.0 superflex ADP is a straight-up draft-bank robbery, and pairing him with Trevor Lawrence gives Stinky Nala a genuinely strong QB/Superflex spine; both sit in NFL.com’s 2026 QB tier with top-10 potential. 7 Brock Bowers, George Kittle, Christian McCaffrey, and Nico Collins are legitimate firepower—Bowers is widely ranked as the TE1, while McCaffrey and Collins remain premium options. 8 But this is a managed half-PPR lineup, not best ball: carrying just five WRs and three RBs leaves almost no injury or bye-week insulation for a format that starts three receivers plus a flex, and the WR room’s league-worst positional standing is the roster’s screaming alarm bell. Spending a roster spot on a second kicker and reaching 28 picks for Denver D/ST is draft-night clutter when this team desperately needed playable RB/WR depth; Denver may be a stout real-life defense, but it cannot catch passes for the thin bench. 9 The TE depth is luxurious, sure, but Stinky Nala built a fancy penthouse kitchen and forgot to stock the fridge—there’s a dangerous starter core here, yet an eighth-place roster because one bad week at WR or RB could turn the lineup into a tire fire.

#7 parkerwilson19

Projected starter points per game: 119.0

Biggest Reach: Isaiah Likely — pick 127, ADP 155.0 (+28.0)

Biggest Value: Justin Herbert — pick 54, ADP 13.0 (-41.0)

Taylor, Chase Brown, and Trey McBride give this squad a legitimately nasty RB/TE spine—the No. 2 RB room and No. 1 TE group can absolutely carry Sundays. Taylor remains a high-end RB1 and Brown brings dependable receiving juice, which plays beautifully in half-PPR. 10
But the last-ranked WR corps is a five-alarm roster-construction problem: Zay Flowers is the only proven weekly hammer, while Luther Burden, Carnell Tate, and Jordyn Tyson leave you praying rookies become lineup locks immediately; Tyson also entered the league with notable injury questions. 11
In a managed superflex league, Herbert at pick 54 versus an adjusted ADP of 13 was a full-on draft robbery, but a ninth-ranked QB room means Stafford and Tua have to cooperate instead of merely existing.
The Isaiah Likely pick at 127 versus ADP 155 was an unnecessary reach for a reserve tight end, especially after a modest 2025 stat line, and Seattle DST adds another thin spot. 12
This team has enough elite muscle to win weeks, but the receiver roulette and shaky QB depth make it feel like a sports car with three bald tires.

#6 don’t stop billievin

Biggest Value: Kyler Murray — pick 73, ADP 52.0 (-21.0)

#5 Beets by Dwight

Biggest Value: Jaxson Dart — pick 63, ADP 24.0 (-39.0)

Beets by Dwight is rocking a real contender core: Jayden Daniels’ rushing ceiling plus Jaxson Dart’s dual-threat breakout profile gives this superflex build serious juice, though Daniels’ recent injury history means the QB room has no safety net. 17
De’Von Achane, Breece Hall, and Puka Nacua are the draft-night fireworks—Achane remains an elite receiving weapon, while Puka is the sort of target hog who can detonate a matchup by himself. 18
The WR depth is wonderfully chaotic for a managed half-PPR lineup: Odunze, Watson, Godwin, Addison, Worthy, and Johnston offer weekly matchup options, but Watson’s health record and the lack of a no-doubt WR2 create plenty of lineup-decision landmines. 19
Tyler Warren gives the tight end group real bite, and the RB room is a strength, but carrying only two quarterbacks in a superflex league is playing chicken with the injury gods—not a best-ball setup where you can just let the app clean up the mess.
Dart was the roster’s big ADP bargain according to the league-adjusted data, while the Addison pick was a full-send reach; this is a sharp, high-upside build that just needs its manager to survive the weekly “which volatile receiver ruins my bench?” roulette wheel.
