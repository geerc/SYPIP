#10 kcoffield

Projected starter points: 1954.0

Biggest Reach: Jalen Hurts — pick 15, VOR rank 65 (+50)

Biggest Value: Travis Etienne — pick 56, VOR rank 20 (-36)

This last-place build has a real Superflex spine: Hurts, Mayfield and Willis give kcoffield three playable QB swings, and Hurts’ rushing-driven ceiling still makes him a premium two-QB asset—even if pick 15 was a brutal VOR overpay. 1 Justin Jefferson, DeVonta Smith and Jameson Williams create a ferocious half-PPR WR room, but in a managed lineup—not best ball—this seven-man receiver surplus cannot rescue an RB corps ranked dead last. James Cook is a legitimate hammer after leading the league in rushing in 2025, while Etienne at pick 56 was the draft’s steal; yet Etienne is splitting Saints first-team work and Pollard is the only remaining RB insulation, so one injury or workload wobble turns the weekly FLEX into a minefield. 2 Loveland gives the third-ranked TE room upside, but Gadsden’s path has narrowed behind David Njoku and Charlie Kolar, leaving this roster dangerously dependent on every marquee starter hitting rather than providing the boring, bankable running-back depth that wins managed leagues. 3

#9 spiro327

Projected starter points: 1969.1

Biggest Reach: Houston Texans — pick 91, VOR rank 513 (+422)

Biggest Value: Chuba Hubbard — pick 110, VOR rank 74 (-36)

This roster has a real superflex foundation—Mahomes, Caleb Williams and Bo Nix give spiro327 weekly quarterback options—but in a managed lineup, that third QB is less valuable than the RB/TE firepower this build skipped. Ja’Marr Chase and Drake London are a legitimate wrecking crew, with London still ESPN’s WR7 in its superflex rankings, but the WR depth behind them is more matchup puzzle than set-it-and-forget-it muscle. 4 The running-back room is where this draft starts leaking oil: Jeanty must be a star immediately, while Swift, Hubbard and especially third-stringer Jonah Coleman leave frighteningly little injury insulation; Hubbard’s value was a sharp scoop, but it cannot patch that structural hole. Houston D/ST at pick 91 was a full-blast reach when this format demands scarce weekly starters, and the TE duo is merely middling—ESPN slots Goedert TE10 and Kincaid TE15. 4 Stribling is an exciting upside swing after a strong preseason, but FantasyPros still has him at WR46 consensus and notes his shoulder issue, which is exactly the kind of volatility that needs bench management—not blind best-ball optimism. 5

#8 Stinky Nala

Projected starter points: 2011.6

Biggest Reach: Denver Broncos — pick 115, VOR rank 518 (+403)

Biggest Value: Rhamondre Stevenson — pick 95, VOR rank 52 (-43)

Stinky Nala has real star power at the top: Christian McCaffrey, Nico Collins and Brock Bowers are premium half-PPR anchors, while Dak Prescott and Trevor Lawrence form a legitimately strong Superflex pairing. 6 But this is a managed lineup, not best ball, and the build wastes precious flexibility on two kickers, a third tight end, and the Broncos DST at a spectacular +403 reach—an outright draft-room fumble. The WR room is the roster-killer: after Collins, Emeka Egbuka is exciting but still volatile, while Terry McLaurin and DK Metcalf carry rebound/role concerns, explaining that ugly ninth-place positional standing. 7 Rhamondre Stevenson at pick 95 was a slick value, but David Montgomery is a touchdown-dependent flex and Stevenson himself faces a workload squeeze, leaving only three backs to cover two RB slots and flex duty. 8 Bowers plus George Kittle gives this team a TE hammer, but Strange is a low-ceiling TE2; the surplus at tight end cannot rescue a thin receiver corps and baffling bench allocation. 9

#7 parkerwilson19

Projected starter points: 2022.4

Biggest Reach: Matthew Stafford — pick 17, VOR rank 115 (+98)

Biggest Value: TreVeyon Henderson — pick 74, VOR rank 45 (-29)

Jonathan Taylor, Chase Brown and Trey McBride are the steel beams here—each sits among FantasyPros’ premium options, and McBride gives this roster a legitimate weekly edge at tight end. 10 But in a half-PPR, managed-lineup superflex, the Matthew Stafford pick was a thunderous overpay: QB depth matters, yet his reach drained capital from a receiver room that ranks dead last and forces volatile calls behind Zay Flowers. Flowers is a real WR2 after a career-best 2025, while Sutton remains a touchdown-driven WR3; that is not enough insulation when Luther Burden, Carnell Tate and Jordyn Tyson are still young bets rather than bankable weekly starters. 11 Henderson at pick 74 was the draft’s clean escape hatch—his receiving chops make him a tasty half-PPR flex lottery ticket—but Monangai is a thin bench stash, not usable depth. 12 This squad can smash if the RB engine carries it, but the managed format exposes every shaky WR decision; it drafted ceiling, then forgot to buy enough floor.

#6 don’t stop billievin

Projected starter points: 2041.2

Biggest Reach: Joe Burrow — pick 18, VOR rank 79 (+61)

Biggest Value: Alec Pierce — pick 108, VOR rank 75 (-33)

Bijan Robinson and Derrick Henry give this roster a terrifying RB foundation—NFL.com still places both among the elite backs, though Henry’s massive career workload makes the age-risk impossible to ignore. 13 In a half-PPR managed lineup with three required WR slots plus a FLEX, however, an RB-heavy bench cannot cover a WR room ranked eighth: Rice is the hammer, but McConkey, Moore, Pierce, and Pittman leave precious little margin for injuries or inconsistency. Alec Pierce at pick 108 was a slick value swing, yet this build needed more than one bargain to compensate for the Burrow reach at pick 18—especially when Kyler’s rushing ceiling already gives the superflex spot real punch. 14 Mark Andrews as the only tight end and bottom-ranked Lions D/ST are construction alarms in a managed league, where you can stream defense but cannot magically replace a weekly TE miss. This is a sixth-place roster with championship-caliber RB force, but it drafted like best ball at receiver—big swings, too few bodies, and a weekly lineup headache waiting to explode.

#5 Beets by Dwight

Projected starter points: 2049.9

Biggest Reach: Jayden Daniels — pick 23, VOR rank 78 (+55)

Biggest Value: J.K. Dobbins — pick 118, VOR rank 58 (-60)

Beets by Dwight earns its top-five shine by pairing Puka Nacua and De’Von Achane—both elite half-PPR consensus options—with an RB room that also includes Breece Hall and the massive-upside rookie Jeremiyah Love. 15 In a superflex managed league, the Jayden Daniels reach is defensible because he’s a top-tier fantasy QB, while Jaxson Dart’s rushing production gives this lineup weekly ceiling rather than just best-ball-only spike appeal. 15 Tyler Warren is a credible tight-end stabilizer, and snagging J.K. Dobbins so far below his value rank is the kind of late-round theft that can juice the RB depth chart. 16 The warning siren: with only two quarterbacks, any Daniels health relapse or Dart regression leaves no superflex safety net, and the WR2/WR3 mix behind Nacua is more boom-bust than dependable. Daniels’ 2025 was injury-shortened, while Dart also missed time as a rookie—this roster needs sharp weekly lineup management, not autopilot. 17

#4 Chowder

Projected starter points: 2060.0

Biggest Reach: Jared Goff — pick 42, VOR rank 123 (+81)

Biggest Value: Bhayshul Tuten — pick 82, VOR rank 40 (-42)

Chowder’s superflex foundation is a sledgehammer: Drake Maye supplies true difference-making QB upside, while Goff and Darnold give the weekly lineup flexibility a managed league actually demands—though ESPN rightly views Goff as more matchup streamer than set-and-forget star. 18 Jaxon Smith-Njigba and A.J. Brown headline a loaded three-WR build, with FantasyPros’ half-PPR VBD placing them among the top 10 wideouts, and LaPorta/Kelce create a luxury tight-end decision rather than a hole. 19 The Tuten pick was a thunderous value swing—current FantasyPros half-PPR VBD has him RB22—but the RB room still carries the roster’s biggest red flag after Kyren Williams, because Dowdle, Mason, and White are more depth darts than dependable RB2 answers. 20 Reaching that far for Goff stings, especially with three quarterbacks already rostered, but the elite receiver firepower, Maye’s ceiling, and strong kicker/DST setup make this a roster built to win weekly choices—not merely pray for best-ball spike weeks.

#3 Panini Sprint Project

Projected starter points: 2064.7

Biggest Reach: Los Angeles Rams — pick 84, VOR rank 548 (+464)

Biggest Value: Bucky Irving — pick 57, VOR rank 33 (-24)

Panini Sprint Project is a superflex sledgehammer: Josh Allen remains ESPN’s QB1, while C.J. Stroud gives this roster a legitimate weekly second-QB starter rather than a desperation flex play. 21 CeeDee Lamb, Tee Higgins, and Davante Adams form a nasty half-PPR receiver spine—Lamb carries elite WR1 upside, while Higgins’ touchdown role and Adams’ red-zone domination make the weekly ceiling vicious. 22 The Bucky Irving value at pick 57 was a draft-room robbery, and pairing him with Omarion Hampton’s emerging lead-back profile gives the RB room real punch despite its middling positional rank. 23 Harold Fannin is the tight-end accelerator after a huge rookie target share, with Hunter Henry supplying a credible safety net. 24 The only real alarm bell is depth: with just four RBs and five WRs in a managed lineup, this build has little margin if the rookies wobble or Adams finally feels his age—and the Rams DST reach was an unnecessary flare shot.

#2 The Spotted Lantern Flies

Projected starter points: 2090.4

Biggest Reach: Jordan Love — pick 72, VOR rank 161 (+89)

Biggest Value: Jadarian Price — pick 92, VOR rank 61 (-31)

The Lantern Flies are a full-blown contender: Gibbs and Barkley supply thunder at RB, while the league’s top-ranked WR room can start Olave, Pickens, Wilson, Nabers, and Harrison without blinking—a half-PPR weekly matchup nightmare. Current expert rankings still place Pickens, Nabers, Olave, and Wilson firmly in the high-end WR conversation, validating both the star power and the absurd depth here. 25 Kraft is a sneaky ceiling engine at tight end after his explosive 2025 pace, and Price at pick 92 was the kind of value swing that can turn a great roster nuclear if Seattle feeds its first-round rookie. 26 The one siren: in a managed-lineup superflex league, QB10 is a dangerous structural soft spot—Love was a reach at 72, and relying on Shough means the lineup lacks the locked-in quarterback insulation elite builds usually demand. Love’s 2025 production was solid but not difference-making enough to erase that concern. 27

#1 lamps❤️

Projected starter points: 2113.2

Biggest Reach: Lamar Jackson — pick 10, VOR rank 53 (+43)

Biggest Value: Josh Jacobs — pick 80, VOR rank 44 (-36)

This is a superflex sledgehammer: Lamar Jackson and Brock Purdy give lamps❤️ a weekly QB edge, while Amon-Ra St. Brown, Tetairoa McMillan, Kenneth Walker and Javonte Williams supply premium half-PPR firepower. ESPN ranks Walker, Williams and Josh Jacobs as top-15 RB options, making Jacobs at pick 80 an outright heist and giving this team rare three-back insulation for a managed lineup. 28 McMillan is already viewed as a breakout candidate after an ultra-busy rookie season, and St. Brown remains an elite consensus asset—this WR room has a true alpha and a rising hammer. 29 The only real yellow flag is fragility beneath the starters: with only two quarterbacks in a superflex league and uncertain depth pieces such as Croskey-Merritt, one injury or role collapse could force uncomfortable lineup decisions. 30 Still, even with Lamar at a rich pick-10 price and a middling TE rank, the Pitts-Ferguson pairing plus Aubrey and Philadelphia’s DST make this roster look like a loaded title favorite, not merely a pretty draft board.
